Most lawn problems have a fix. This one often does not, and it is better to know that early than to spend three seasons fighting it.

Bermuda is a full-sun grass. Not a grass that prefers sun — a grass that requires it. Where it does not get enough light, it thins, and no amount of water, feed or attention changes that.

## How much light it actually needs

Bermuda wants something in the region of six to eight hours of direct sunlight a day through the growing season. Direct is the operative word. Bright shade, dappled light under a canopy, or a few hours of weak early sun do not count the same way.

Below that, the plant cannot make enough energy to sustain dense growth. It gets leggy as it stretches for light, the canopy opens up, and the lawn thins from the inside. What you see is grass that looks sparse no matter what you do, with soil showing through.

## What a shaded Bermuda lawn looks like

The pattern is recognisable once you know it.

It is worst on the north side of the house and under tree canopies, and it follows the shade rather than following anything else — the line between good grass and thin grass tracks where the shadow falls through the day.

It gets worse through the season rather than better, because as trees fill out in spring the light gets worse, not more generous.

And it does not respond to the usual remedies. Fertilising a shaded lawn does not thicken it; it mostly produces soft, stretched growth that thins again. Watering more makes it wetter, not denser. If an area of lawn has resisted everything you have thrown at it, measure the light before trying anything else.

## Watching the light honestly

People are generally poor judges of how much sun a part of their yard gets, because we see it at a handful of moments rather than across a day.

Pick a clear day and look at the area at mid-morning, noon, mid-afternoon and late afternoon. Write down whether it is in direct sun or shade each time. Four observations tell you more than an impression does.

Do it in the growing season, with the trees in leaf. A spot that gets good light in February under bare branches may get almost none in June, and June is when it matters.

## What you can change

Sometimes the light is fixable, and when it is, that is the real repair.

Thinning a tree canopy — raising the crown, taking out some interior growth — can let considerably more light through without removing the tree. That is a genuine fix rather than a workaround, and it addresses the cause.

Structures and fences are harder, because the shadow is permanent and moves with the season.

Where the light can be improved, improve it first and then worry about the grass. Where it cannot, the honest answer is that Bermuda is not going to hold there.

## What to do with the spots that will not take

This is the part nobody likes, but it saves money.

A stretch of yard that gets three hours of filtered light is not a Bermuda spot and will not become one. Repeatedly laying new sod into it is an expensive way to keep learning the same lesson — fresh sod will look good for a while on the strength of what it arrived with, then thin out exactly as the last lot did.

Those areas are better handled as something other than lawn. Beds, mulch, ground cover, stepping stones, or simply accepting a thinner patch under a tree are all better outcomes than an annual re-sod.

It is worth saying plainly: deep shade is not a failure of maintenance and it is not something you did wrong. It is a site condition, and the grass is just telling you the truth about it.

## Why this matters more at the start

If you are planning a new lawn, this is the first thing to work out, before ground prep, before quantities, before anything. Map the sun across a full day, mark the areas that genuinely get six hours or more, and plan the lawn to fit the light you actually have rather than the lawn you pictured.

Bermuda laid where it gets what it needs is one of the toughest, most forgiving lawn grasses available here. Laid where it does not, it will struggle no matter how well it is looked after — and that is not a problem better maintenance can solve.

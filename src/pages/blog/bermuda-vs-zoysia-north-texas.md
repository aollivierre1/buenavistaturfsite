Most people asking this question have already decided they want a good-looking lawn. The real question underneath is which grass will still look good in August, and which one fits how they actually live.

## Start with sun, not preference

This is the single factor that decides more lawns than any other. Bermuda needs strong, direct light — six to eight hours minimum. If your yard has mature trees, structures casting long shadows, or a north-facing stretch that never really brightens up, Bermuda will thin out no matter how well you maintain it.

Zoysia handles moderate shade considerably better. So does St Augustine, which is the specialist when shade is truly the governing condition.

## Traffic and recovery

If kids, dogs, or equipment cross the lawn regularly, Bermuda recovers faster. It spreads aggressively and fills its own wear patterns during the growing season. Zoysia is denser but slower to repair — damage shows longer.

For athletic fields and heavy-use commercial entrances, Bermuda is usually the answer.

## Mowing and maintenance

Zoysia grows more slowly, which means less mowing. Bermuda grows fast in peak summer and will look ragged if you skip weeks. That is a real lifestyle consideration, not a minor detail.

## Cost

Bermuda is the value play. Zoysia is a premium product at a premium price. Both are worth it in the right yard — neither is worth it in the wrong one.

## The short version

- **Full sun, active use, value-focused** — Bermuda 419
- **Full sun, premium appearance** — Celebration or Tahoma 31
- **Mixed sun and shade, premium finish, less mowing** — Zeon Zoysia
- **Heavy shade** — St Augustine
